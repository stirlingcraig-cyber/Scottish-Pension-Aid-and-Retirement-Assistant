# Cairn — Pension Guidance for Scotland

Cairn is an educational pension-guidance app for people in Scotland. It walks a user through short question modules — a fact-find, contributions & tax relief, pot & projections, State Pension & National Insurance, retirement income options, and beneficiaries & death benefits — plus a flagship "Full pension forecast" calculator.

The forecast calculator runs recognised pension maths client-side: compound-growth pot projections across cautious/balanced/optimistic scenarios, an optional 25% tax-free lump sum on defined contribution pots (toggle per pension), an illustrative annuity conversion, the commonly-cited 4% sustainable drawdown rule, and a State Pension estimate from National Insurance qualifying years. It supports households of one or two people (each identified by date of birth, for precise age-related calculations), each with multiple pensions of different types (defined contribution, defined benefit, already-in-payment, and a dedicated Police Scotland public-sector pathway), plus savings, and projects any income gap against a stated annual required income in retirement. It also calculates, per person, the earliest retirement age at which their stated target income is sustainably achievable.

The Police Scotland pathway correctly models the 2015 pension reforms: pre-2015 service accrues under the legacy 2006 final-salary scheme, 2015–2022 remedy-period service is resolved via the McCloud "deferred choice underpin", and service from April 2022 onward is mandatory 2015 CARE accrual.

Guidance text is normally written by an AI guidance engine, but the app also ships a fully self-contained, rule-based fallback: if the AI service can't be reached (as on a static host with no API key), it automatically falls back to built-in plain-English explanations instead of showing an error — the pension maths and figures are identical either way. It correctly models the Scottish income tax bands and the relief-at-source nuance for Scottish taxpayers. It is aimed at UK consumers in Scotland researching their pension before speaking to a regulated adviser — it is educational guidance only, not a personal recommendation or regulated financial advice.
